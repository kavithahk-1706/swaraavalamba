About

What is Swarāvalamba?

A real-time Carnatic music practice companion that tracks your hand gestures and voice simultaneously.

The Problem

Every Carnatic music student faces the same challenge: learning to maintain tālam (rhythmic hand gestures) while singing swaras accurately in shruti. These are two independent cognitive tasks that must become second nature — and traditional practice relies entirely on a teacher's real-time correction, which isn't available during solo practice at home.

Students often develop one skill at the expense of the other — their tālam drifts when they focus on pitch, or their swaras waver when they concentrate on hand movements. Without immediate feedback, bad habits go unnoticed and become harder to fix.

The Approach

Swarāvalamba uses your webcam to track hand landmarks via MediaPipe Hand Landmarker and your microphone to detect pitch via the Web Audio API with autocorrelation-based pitch detection.

For tālam, the system extracts a few key signals from your hand: finger-to-thumb distances (for counting), tip-drop values (for palm/lap mode), wrist height (for beat detection), and palm orientation (for wave/flip detection). These are classified into actions — finger touch, beat, wave, or idle — and matched against the expected tāḷa cycle.

For swaras, detected frequencies are mapped to the nearest Carnatic swara given your base shruti, with raga-aware naming that handles the 16-to-12 swara mapping (overlapping pairs like Ri2/Ga1). Scoring uses cents deviation with ±33-cent tolerance for fast passages, plus weighted partial credit for near-misses.

What's Available Today

Swarāvalamba is not tied to one tāḷa: the aim is to support any tāḷa, together with swaras sung in any shruti. These are the pieces that exist right now:

Exercises — Sarali 1, Sarali 2, Sarali 3 and Dātu Swara.
Tāḷa — Ādi tāḷam (8 aksharas per cycle) is currently the only tāḷa.
Shruti — Sa can be set anywhere from C to B.

Three Tala Modes

Air mode — the hand moves freely in the air; finger-to-thumb proximity is used for counting. This is the most reliable mode currently.

Palm mode — the hand counts on the other palm; tip-drop values detect which finger lifts. Requires the system to track the correct hand.

Lap mode — the hand counts on the lap; similar tip-drop detection but with different baseline calibration.

Swara Mapping

Carnatic music uses 16 swara names mapped to 12 semitones. Four overlapping pairs share a semitone — which name to use depends on the raga. The system supports raga-aware naming for 7 melakartas including Māyāmāḷavagowḷa, Shankarābharaṇam, Kharaharapriya, Hanumatōḍi, Kalyāṇi, Harikāmbhōji, and Naṭabhairavi.

Speed Levels

1st speed — 1 swara per beat. The fundamental tempo.
2nd speed — 2 swaras per beat. Requires faster articulation.
3rd speed — 4 swaras per beat. Advanced, with half-beat split scoring to compensate for ~500ms singer latency.

Origin

Swarāvalamba was presented at Swara Tank during the Chowdiah Sangeetha Mela 2026 in Bengaluru — a pitch competition for music-tech ideas. The name comes from Sanskrit: svara (musical note) + avalamba (support/companion), meaning "support through swaras" — a practice companion that helps you become self-reliant in your music.
